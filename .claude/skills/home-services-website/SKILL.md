# Home Services Website

Collects the brief for a trades or home services business, then hands off to `website-launch` for the build and deployment.

## How to run this

1. Interview the user with the questions below — use `AskUserQuestion` for the discrete choices.
2. Confirm the brief back in a short summary.
3. Invoke `website-launch` and follow its workflow from Step 2 (design system) onward.

## Intake questions

**Required before building:**

1. Business name, city, country, base address
2. Trade — plumbing, electrical, HVAC/AC, cleaning, handyman, painting, pest control, landscaping, moving
3. Language(s) — if Arabic, full RTL layout
4. **Service areas** — the specific neighbourhoods, cities, or radius covered
5. Services offered
6. Phone and WhatsApp — the primary conversion channels in this category
7. Hours, and whether they offer emergency or 24/7 callout
8. Pricing approach — fixed price list, call-out fee plus hourly, or free quote
9. Licences, insurance, certifications, and any trade body membership
10. Domain — owned or needs buying?

**Ask if relevant:**

11. Response time they can genuinely commit to
12. Guarantees or warranty on work
13. Residential, commercial, or both
14. Team size — a solo trader and a twelve-van operation need different positioning
15. Payment methods accepted

## What actually converts for home services

This category converts by **phone call**, not by form. Someone with a leak is not filling in a contact form — design accordingly.

1. **The phone number is the website.** Large, tappable, in the header, in the hero, sticky on mobile, repeated on every page. Everything else is secondary.
2. **Urgency is the buying trigger.** Much of this traffic is an emergency. "Available now", "same-day callout", and a genuine response-time promise convert far better than a company history.
3. **Trust signals overcome stranger-in-my-home anxiety.** Licensed, insured, background-checked, uniformed, named staff with photos. This is a real and rational fear, especially for customers who are home alone — address it explicitly.
4. **Price transparency beats competitors who hide it.** Even a call-out fee and an hourly rate reduces the fear of being overcharged, which is the single biggest hesitation in this industry.
5. **Service-area pages are the entire local SEO strategy.** "Emergency plumber [neighbourhood]" is the search that matters. One page per major area, with genuinely local content — not the same paragraph with the place name swapped, which search engines discount and readers see through.
6. **Reviews carry disproportionate weight.** Google reviews matter more than the website's own testimonials. Link to the Google Business Profile and make review-gathering part of the handover advice.

## Page structure

**Home** — hero with the phone number, service area, and emergency availability immediately visible · services grid · why choose us (licensed, insured, guaranteed) · pricing or how quoting works · service areas · reviews · how it works, step by step · booking/quote CTA · sticky mobile bar with Call and WhatsApp, present at all times

**Other pages** — One page per major service · One page per service area · About and team · Pricing · Reviews · Contact and quote request · Emergency callout

## Industry rules

- **Display licence and insurance details** where the trade requires them. For electrical, gas, and pest control work this is frequently a legal requirement, and customers actively check.
- **Never invent certifications, licence numbers, insurance cover, or trade body membership.** Claiming a gas or electrical qualification the business does not hold is a safety issue and a criminal offence in many jurisdictions.
- **Never invent reviews.** Fake reviews are illegal in a growing number of jurisdictions and directly actionable.
- **Emergency and response-time claims must be honest.** Do not put "24/7" on the site unless someone genuinely answers at 3am — a broken promise here generates complaints and chargebacks.
- **Quoted prices need clear scope.** State what a call-out fee covers, whether parts are included, and whether evenings and weekends cost more.
- **Guarantees must be specific.** "Satisfaction guaranteed" is meaningless; "12-month warranty on workmanship" is real.

## Design direction

Clear, fast, and utterly unambiguous. This is not a category that rewards subtlety — the visitor is stressed, often on a phone, sometimes standing in water.

Big type, high contrast, an unmissable phone button, and near-zero visual clutter. Fast load time matters more than aesthetic refinement; someone with an emergency will not wait for a hero video.

Trustworthy over trendy. A clean, slightly conventional site with real photos of the actual team and vans outperforms a stylish abstract one. Photos of real staff in branded uniform beside real vehicles are the strongest trust signal available and cost nothing to take.
